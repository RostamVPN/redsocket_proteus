## 1. Executive Summary

redsocket_proteus is the RostamVPN exit server placed behind Proteus programmable protocols. Its only listener is `[proteus]`: every accepted TCP connection is driven by a Proteus interpreter compiled at start-up from a protocol specification file (PSF), and the Noise NK tunnel of redsocket_http runs unchanged on the interpreter's application side. Two PSFs ship: `tls_mimic.psf`, a fixed TLS-1.2-shaped handshake followed by TLS-record-shaped data, and `shadowsocks.psf`, ChaCha20-Poly1305 frames under a password written into the file. The HTTP Upgrade front, the WebSocket codec, the 404 decoy and the io_uring relay of the predecessor are gone.

Overall posture. The integration layer (`src/proteus_carrier.rs`, 535 lines) is small and disciplined: PSFs are compiled once with the parser's panics converted to start-up errors, dispatch rules are explicit and tested, shedding happens before any allocation, the interpreter's lifetime is bounded by a 600 s budget plus a 30 s grace and a forced abort, nothing is ever answered in clear, and the PROXY-protocol default is now safe. The risk sits in what the vendored interpreter — described by its own README as "experimental and not recommended for security-critical applications" — and the two shipped PSFs do with hostile, or merely ordinary, Internet traffic. Red Team Ltd found that (1) with the example configuration, any 18 bytes that do not authenticate under the shadowsocks password make the vendored interpreter `unwrap()` a failed decryption, which under the shipped `panic = "abort"` profile terminates the exit process and every tunnel on it — a condition triggered by any browser, scanner or probe that reaches the port; (2) `tls_mimic.psf` is a byte-for-byte copy of a public upstream test fixture whose handshake violates the TLS state machine, carries 2013 timestamps and an expired 1024-bit SHA-1 test certificate, and answers its published 166-byte ClientHello with a deterministic 746-byte reply; (3) `shadowsocks.psf` keys every connection with `SHA-256(password)` and restarts a fixed nonce sequence per connection, so the first 18 bytes in each direction are a per-deployment constant, nonces repeat across connections, and a captured first frame can be replayed as a key-less probe. The Noise NK layer inside is unchanged and remains sound; the problems are in the shaping layer the product now relies on for censorship resistance.

Finding counts: 7 new findings — Critical 1, High 2, Medium 1, Low 3. Of the 17 findings of the redsocket_http assessment (RVPN-SRV-001 to -017), 4 are resolved in this tree, 3 are narrowed and 10 are inherited unchanged; they are cited by ID in Section 5.8 rather than repeated.

Top three issues
1. RVPN-PRX-001 (Critical) — Any 18 non-authenticating bytes abort the process: `unwrap()` on a failed MAC in the vendored interpreter under `panic = "abort"`; reproduced out of process (SIGABRT, exit 134).
2. RVPN-PRX-002 (High) — `tls_mimic.psf` is a published, static, protocol-violating handshake and a one-probe oracle.
3. RVPN-PRX-003 (High) — `shadowsocks.psf` has a static key and a fixed nonce sequence: constant 18-byte opener per direction, nonce reuse across connections, replayable first frame.

Headline recommendation. Do not deploy this tree to an Internet-facing node until the `unwrap()` paths in `third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs` propagate errors and a release-profile test proves a node survives garbage input; then treat both shipped PSFs as development fixtures and replace them with carriers that have per-connection randomness, a complete and conformant imitation of the protocol they claim to be, and a probe response indistinguishable from a real service. The inherited identity issue (RVPN-SRV-002) still gates enabling `[messaging]` on a public exit.

### 2.2 Attack-surface summary (from the manifests and configuration, before code review)

- Inbound, unauthenticated: TCP `:443` and every `extra_binds` address → shedding check → optional PROXY header → peek of the first bytes (1 KiB window, 5 s) → dispatch: the longest fixed client prefix that matches wins (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:105-108`, `:137-145`); otherwise the one PSF whose first client message has no fixed opening (`:149-152`); otherwise close (`:339-341`); two prefix-less PSFs are refused at start-up (`:185-193`) → the selected PSF's interpreter → Noise NK (authenticates the server only; the public key ships in every client) → client-asserted META (`tz`, `app`, `os`, `cid`, `sni`, `caps`) → CONNECT / UDP ASSOCIATE / `_rs.*`.
- Inbound, loopback by default: Prometheus text endpoint `127.0.0.1:9090` (`src/config.rs:253-316`).
- Outbound: DNS (hickory, system configuration); TCP/UDP to any destination not in `deny_cidrs`, bound to a per-`cid` sticky egress IP; TLS to the stats Valkey; TLS/mTLS to the messaging/protection Valkey.
- Trusted inputs: `config.toml`; the PSF files named in `[proteus] psfs` (a PSF programs the wire protocol and is compiled into the process at start-up — it is trusted code, not data); `SIGHUP`; the Ed25519-signed protection bundle; and, unsigned, the Valkey mailbox/rules/ledger hashes.
- Removed relative to redsocket_http: the HTTP Upgrade handler, the WebSocket codec and the 404 decoy (`README.md:13-19`), the io_uring relay (the `io-uring` crate is absent from `Cargo.lock`; "the relay is the tokio relay on every platform", `README.md:60`), the `[http] proteus_psf(s)` same-port mode (an `[http]` table is ignored, `src/config.rs:391-422`), and the `mux_client`/`legacy_client`/`flooder` binaries.

## 3. Severity Methodology

Severity is Impact × Likelihood, calibrated to the engagement threat model (a state-level network operator who can observe, probe and block at national scale, run hostile networks, seize devices and compromise bootstrap or relay infrastructure; secondary adversaries are ordinary network attackers and a hostile tunnel client or hostile exit).

Impact: Critical — deanonymisation of users at scale, remote compromise of the exit, exposure of other users' traffic, or — amended for this report — an unauthenticated, single-connection termination of the exit process and every tunnel on it that is repeatable at will and is also triggered incidentally by ordinary Internet traffic, so that an Internet-facing node cannot remain in service and the primary adversary obtains a fleet-wide kill switch. High — enables blocking of the service fleet-wide (fingerprint or probe oracle), cross-user authorisation breach, or node-wide outage caused by an unauthenticated remote party that requires sustained volume. Medium — single-user integrity or billing manipulation, bounded denial of service, disclosure of infrastructure or load information, spoofable attribution. Low — hygiene and defence in depth.

The amendment is stated here so that this report and the redsocket_http report, which places volume-dependent node-wide outage (RVPN-SRV-003) at High, remain consistent: RVPN-PRX-001 ranks above RVPN-SRV-003 because it needs 18 bytes rather than sustained traffic and is triggered by traffic the node will receive without any adversary.

Likelihood: High — unauthenticated, trivial, default configuration. Medium — needs volume, timing, knowledge of an identifier, or a non-default but documented configuration. Low — needs a privileged position (Valkey write access, local host access, a compromised component, a non-shipped PSF).

| Impact \ Likelihood | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Critical | Critical | High |
| High | High | High | Medium |
| Medium | Medium | Medium | Low |
| Low | Low | Low | Informational |

CVSS 3.1 base vectors are given for comparability only; CVSS does not model censorship resistance or fleet-wide consequences and the matrix above takes precedence.

## 5. Detailed Findings

### RVPN-PRX-001 — Any 18 non-authenticating bytes abort the exit process: `unwrap()` on a failed MAC in the vendored interpreter under the shipped `panic = "abort"` profile

- Severity: Critical. Impact Critical under the amendment in Section 3: one TCP connection with 18 arbitrary bytes terminates the process and every tunnel on it (worst outcome B for every connected user), repeatable at will (a fleet-wide kill switch, functionally outcome C), and triggered incidentally by any ordinary TLS or HTTP client that reaches port 443, so a node running the example configuration cannot stay in service. Likelihood High (example configuration; no key, no handshake, no volume). CVSS 3.1: AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:N/I:N/A:H (7.5) — CVSS does not model the fleet-wide and tunnel-loss consequences.
- Status: CONFIRMED. Reproduced out of process (Appendix C-0): a server-role interpreter compiled from `proteus/shadowsocks.psf`, driven exactly as `src/proteus_carrier.rs:350-365` drives it, was fed `GET / HTTP/1.1\r\nHo` (18 bytes) and panicked at `third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs:267:54` with `called Result::unwrap() on an Err value: Crypto(Decrypt(Decrypt(Error)))`; the same program built with `panic = "abort"` terminated with SIGABRT (exit status 134). The server binary was not run.
- Location: `third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs:243-278` (`:267`); `third_party/proteus/src/crypto/chacha.rs:195-206`; `third_party/proteus/src/lang/compiler/mod.rs:665-666`, `:746-754`; `src/proteus_carrier.rs:149-152`, `:350-365`; `Cargo.toml:61-66`; `proteus/shadowsocks.psf:6-10`, `:28-30`; `config.example.toml:29`; `README.md:210-215`; `scripts/proteus-e2e.sh:50`, `:197-201`; `src/bin/client.rs:34-45`.
- Description. For the server role the compiler emits, for every incoming `EncDataMsg`, a `ReadExact` of the fixed prefix — `length` (2 bytes) and `length_mac` (16 bytes) (`compiler/mod.rs:665-666`) — followed by `DecryptField` of `length` with `length_mac` as the tag (`:746-754`). `DecryptFieldArgs::execute` calls `runtime.decrypt(&ciphertext, &mac_fixed).unwrap()` (`vm.rs:267`); `DecryptionCipher::decrypt` returns `Err(CipherError::Decrypt)` whenever the Poly1305 tag does not verify (`chacha.rs:202-205`). The `unwrap()` therefore panics on any 18 bytes not produced under the PSF's password. The carrier runs the interpreter as a plain tokio task with no panic handling (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:358-363`), and none could help: the release profile sets `panic = "abort"` (`Cargo.toml:65`), which terminates the process. In the example configuration (`config.example.toml:29`: `tls_mimic.psf`, then `shadowsocks.psf` as the default) every connection whose first bytes are not the 166-byte `tls_mimic` ClientHello is routed to `shadowsocks.psf` (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:149-152`), so the trigger is any client that sends 18 bytes: Internet scanners, a browser's ClientHello, an HTTP client, or a censor's probe. The repository's own evidence that garbage "is closed at once" (`README.md:210-215`) was produced under the unwind strategy: `scripts/proteus-e2e.sh:50` builds the debug profile and asserts "connection closed" at `:197-201`, and `cargo test` runs the debug profile too. Under unwind the panic ends only the task (observed: `JoinError::is_panic() == true`, the peer sees a zero-byte close), which matches the documentation; under the profile that ships, it ends the node.
- Evidence (`third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs:264-267`):
  ```rust
  let mut mac_fixed = [0u8; 16];
  mac_fixed.copy_from_slice(&mac);

  runtime.decrypt(&ciphertext, &mac_fixed).unwrap()
  ```
  `Cargo.toml:61-66`: `[profile.release]` … `panic = "abort"`. Observed output of Appendix C-0 under `panic = "abort"`: `thread 'tokio-rt-worker' panicked at .../third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs:267:54: called Result::unwrap() on an Err value: Crypto(Decrypt(Decrypt(Error)))`, then process exit status 134; the line printed after the join in the program never appeared.
- Impact. (a) Every established tunnel on the node ends at once and clients fall into the reconnect window. (b) A censor kills every exit it can reach with 18 bytes each and repeats it on a timer — a fleet-wide block that needs no DPI and no key. (c) The abort is also triggered without any adversary: a node configured per `config.example.toml` on a public address is restarted by background Internet traffic. (d) The same instruction runs in the standalone client's net-to-app direction: `src/bin/client.rs:34-45` spawns the interpreter without any handler and the client binary shares the release profile, so a hostile or mis-keyed exit speaking `shadowsocks.psf` terminates the CLI client with its first 18 bytes (hostile-exit angle of scope area 6).
- Reproduction. Lab only: `cargo build --release`; start the server with `config.example.toml` (password written into the PSF) on loopback; `printf 'GET / HTTP/1.1\r\nHo' | nc 127.0.0.1 443`; observe SIGABRT. Appendix C-0 reproduces the panic without running the server.
- Recommendation. (1) In the vendored crate, replace the `unwrap()` at `vm.rs:267` (and `:270`, `:294`, `:305`) with error propagation so a failed tag becomes `lang::Error::Crypto` and the forwarder's existing `handle_error` path (`interpreter/mod.rs:271-296`) closes the connection; record the edit with the three documented in `README.md:272-281`. (2) Treat a failed first-frame authentication as a probe: hold the connection silently for a randomised interval before closing (RVPN-PRX-004). (3) Add a release-profile test — run `scripts/proteus-e2e.sh` against a `--release` build, or an integration test that sends non-authenticating bytes and then opens a real tunnel — so the shipped panic strategy is exercised. (4) Until (1) ships, do not deploy a configuration whose default PSF performs authenticated decryption on the first bytes; a `tls_mimic`-only configuration closes unmatched bytes before any interpreter runs (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:149-152`, `:339-341`) and is not affected by this finding.
- References: ASVS v4.0.3 V7.4.1, V6.2.1, V11.1.4; CWE-248 (Uncaught Exception), CWE-755, CWE-400; The Cargo Book, profile `panic` setting.

### RVPN-PRX-002 — `tls_mimic.psf` is a published, static, protocol-violating TLS-shaped handshake and a deterministic active-probing oracle

- Severity: High. Impact High (a static passive fingerprint and a one-probe confirmation let the primary adversary block every node using this PSF — outcome C). Likelihood High (the bytes are public; no secret is needed for passive or active detection). CVSS 3.1: AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:L/I:N/A:N (5.3) — CVSS understates censorship impact.
- Status: CONFIRMED for the byte shapes and protocol-level violations (derived mechanically from the PSF, Appendix C-1); detection by a named DPI product SUSPECTED pending C-1/C-2.
- Location: `proteus/tls_mimic.psf:6-15`, `:19-25`, `:27-29`, `:33-36`; `third_party/proteus/tests/fixtures/tls_mimic.psf` (identical apart from the SPDX header); `src/proteus_carrier.rs:66-82`, `:134-153`; `README.md:233-235`, `:248`; `tests/harness/src/lib.rs:25-27`.
- Description. The handshake is two fixed byte strings — a 166-byte ClientHello (`tls_mimic.psf:19-20`) answered by a 746-byte blob (`:22-25`) containing ServerHello, Certificate, ServerKeyExchange and ServerHelloDone — after which both sides send `17 03 03 | u16 length | payload` records (`:12-15`, `:27-29`, `:35-36`). Decoding the bytes gives: (1) Sequence violation: after ServerHelloDone the client's next bytes are application-data records; there is no ClientKeyExchange, ChangeCipherSpec or Finished in either direction — a state-machine violation that a TLS-aware DPI engine detects without any signature database. (2) Fixed randoms: both `ClientHello.random` and `ServerHello.random` begin `52 36 2c 10`, a `gmt_unix_time` of 1379281936 = 2013-09-15T21:52:16Z, identical on every connection from every client and node. (3) The certificate is a 441-byte X.509 v1 self-signed `CN=Test Certificate RSA`, RSA 1024-bit, `sha1WithRSAEncryption`, valid 2013-09-15 to 2023-09-13 (expired), serial `F4A72FD3E8FC37C4`, SHA-256 `843e734f3a1f0917a784c34aa081afd5ff5e498bd1ea283fede52e8591db99be`; the secp256r1 point and the 128-byte RSA signature in ServerKeyExchange are equally constant, which no real server reproduces twice. (4) The ServerHello selects compression method `0x01`, which the ClientHello did not offer (it offered only `0x00`); a conformant peer would abort with `illegal_parameter` (RFC 5246 §7.4.1.3). (5) The ClientHello carries no `server_name` extension, one cipher suite (`0xc030`) plus the renegotiation SCSV, and the extension set `11-10-35-13-15`; its JA3 string is `771,49200-255,11-10-35-13-15,14-13-25-11-12-24-9-10-22-23-8-6-7-20-21-4-5-18-19-1-2-3-15-16-17,0-1-2`, MD5 `d092a941ea714ee8a125b386f788d07a`, the same for every client. (6) Data records may declare up to 65535 bytes (the `u16` length; `README.md:248`), whereas TLS 1.2 bounds a record at 2^14 + 2048 bytes (RFC 5246 §6.2.3); every relay chunk above 18432 bytes is a further violation, and record sizes mirror the inner Noise frames (two 50-byte "records" for the handshake, then yamux-sized records). (7) The file is byte-identical to the upstream fixture published in the Proteus repository, so the exchange is public and already associated with Proteus. Active probing: the dispatcher routes exactly these 166 bytes to the PSF (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:134-153`), the fixed bytes are not otherwise verified on receipt, and the server-role interpreter answers with the 746 bytes before anything is authenticated. Sending the published ClientHello to any address yields a deterministic 746-byte confirmation that no TLS server would produce.
- Evidence (`proteus/tls_mimic.psf:33-36`):
  ```
  { ROLE: CLIENT; PHASE: HANDSHAKE; FORMAT: ClientHello };
  { ROLE: SERVER; PHASE: HANDSHAKE; FORMAT: ServerHello };
  { ROLE: CLIENT; PHASE: DATA;      FORMAT: DataMsg };
  { ROLE: SERVER; PHASE: DATA;      FORMAT: DataMsg };
  ```
  `:27-29`: `DataMsg` = `FIXED_BYTES(0x170303)`, `LENGTH`, `PAYLOAD`. Items (1)-(6) decoded from the bytes at `:20` and `:24` by the script in Appendix C-1.
- Impact. A censor can write a passive rule on any of items (1)-(6) — the sequence violation and the 2013 timestamp are the cheapest — confirm a suspect address with one probe, and, because every deployment shares the bytes, block every RostamVPN node using this PSF with one rule. The 101/404 oracle of the HTTP front (RVPN-SRV-001) has been replaced by one that is simpler and already public.
- Reproduction. Appendix C-1 (decode) and C-2 (replay the bytes at `tls_mimic.psf:20` to a node; expect the 746 bytes at `:24`). For comparison `openssl s_client -connect <node>:443` sends a real ClientHello, which is routed to the default PSF (RVPN-PRX-001) or closed.
- Recommendation. Do not deploy `tls_mimic.psf` as shipped. A TLS-shaped carrier needs either a real TLS stack or a faithful imitation: a current browser-class ClientHello with SNI and per-connection randoms, a current certificate chain, a complete handshake, records bounded at 16 KiB, and a probe response that is a genuine TLS alert or a real site. If a Proteus PSF is kept for shaping, use `Random(32)` semantics for the randoms, remove the published fixture bytes, and gate the handshake on a secret only legitimate clients hold.
- References: ASVS v4.0.3 V14.3.3, V9.1.2; CWE-203, CWE-330, CWE-295; RFC 5246 §6.2.3, §7.4.1.3, §7.4.9.

### RVPN-PRX-003 — `shadowsocks.psf`: static key, fixed nonce sequence — constant 18-byte opener per direction, nonce reuse across connections, replayable first frame, placeholder password accepted

- Severity: High. Impact High (passive fingerprint of every connection and a key-less replay oracle — outcome C; the outer layer's integrity is nil, with confidentiality bounded by the Noise layer inside). Likelihood High (deterministic; no secret needed to fingerprint or probe). CVSS 3.1: AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:L/I:L/A:N (6.5).
- Status: CONFIRMED. Determinism traced in code and observed out of process (Appendix C-3): two independent client-role connections, each with a different random 48-byte first application write, produced byte-identical first 18 bytes (`3844da039c759d1d7e1b39b1f95fc2e055d8` for the scratch password) and differing remaining bytes; the server role gave the same result (`52b30808615590dca71d0585fb8852bbba66`). Replay acceptance is traced in code; its on-wire form is test C-3(b).
- Location: `third_party/proteus/src/crypto/kdf.rs:1-5`; `third_party/proteus/src/crypto/chacha.rs:7-8`, `:94-108`, `:132-137`, `:144-166`, `:183-188`, `:195-206`; `third_party/proteus/src/lang/interpreter/vm.rs:345-358`; `third_party/proteus/src/lang/compiler/mod.rs:126-141`; `src/proteus_carrier.rs:165-173`, `:358-363`; `proteus/shadowsocks.psf:22-30`; `README.md:180-182`, `:236-238`; `config.example.toml:27-29`; `src/bin/client.rs:81-86`, `:99-117`.
- Description. The key is `SHA-256(PASSWORD)`: `derive_key_256` ignores its salt parameter (`kdf.rs:1-5`; the caller passes the constant `"stupid stupid stupid"`, `vm.rs:347`). Nonces are neither counters nor random: `generate_nonce` takes 12 bytes from a Salsa20 keystream seeded with the same key and a fixed 8-byte nonce — `0xAA…` for the client-to-server direction, `0xBB…` for the reverse (`chacha.rs:7-8`, `:96-97`, `:104-108`, `:132-137`, `:183-188`). The cipher is constructed anew for every connection by the PSF's init task (`compiler/mod.rs:126-141` → `InitFixedSharedKey`, `vm.rs:345-358`) and the carrier creates one interpreter per socket (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:358-363`), so every connection restarts the nonce stream: frame i of every connection in a given direction is encrypted under the same (key, nonce) on every client and node sharing the PSF copy. Consequences: (1) Constant opener. The first encrypted field is the `u16` length of the first frame, whose plaintext is fixed for a given client implementation (the standalone client's first write is the 2-byte Noise length prefix and the 48-byte msg1, `src/bin/client.rs:81-86`, `:99-117`), so the first 18 bytes on the wire — ciphertext of the length and its tag — are a per-deployment constant; the server's reply opener (length of the 48-byte msg2) is likewise constant. `README.md:236-238` ("Nothing fixed on the wire") is contradicted. (2) Nonce reuse. ChaCha20-Poly1305 under a repeated (key, nonce) leaks the XOR of plaintexts and the Poly1305 key; after two observed connections an observer can forge outer frames. Confidentiality damage is bounded because the payload is Noise ciphertext, but the outer layer then provides neither confidentiality nor integrity of its own. (3) No replay protection or key agreement. A captured first client frame re-sent to any node is accepted — the tag verifies under the same key and nonce — its payload is forwarded as Noise msg1, and the node answers a fresh msg2 inside its constant 18-byte opener: a probe oracle that needs no password. (4) The password is a static shared secret in every client's copy of the file (`README.md:180-182`) and offers no probe resistance against an adversary who holds one client. (5) The server accepts the shipped placeholder: nothing in `Dispatch::load` (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:165-173`) refuses `PASSWORD = "REPLACE_WITH_PASSWORD"` (`shadowsocks.psf:24`); a node started with the unedited file keys the outer layer with `SHA-256("REPLACE_WITH_PASSWORD")`, which is public, and only a comment warns (`config.example.toml:27-28`). Real shadowsocks AEAD derives a per-connection subkey from a random salt sent in clear and counts nonces from zero per subkey, so no two connections share bytes; this PSF reproduces the frame layout but not the property that makes it random-looking.
- Evidence (`third_party/proteus/src/crypto/kdf.rs:1-4`, `chacha.rs:104-108`):
  ```rust
  pub fn derive_key_256(password: &str, _: &str) -> [u8; 32] {
      let mut output_key_material = [0u8; 32];
      output_key_material.clone_from_slice(hex::decode(sha256::digest(password)).unwrap().as_slice());
  ```
  ```rust
  fn generate_nonce(&mut self) -> [u8; 12] {
      let mut buf: [u8; 12] = [0x00; 12];
      self.nonce_gen.apply_keystream(&mut buf);
      buf
  }
  ```
  Observed (Appendix C-3, client role, two connections, random first writes): A `3844da039c759d1d7e1b39b1f95fc2e055d8 b736b2…`, B `3844da039c759d1d7e1b39b1f95fc2e055d8 3b3080…`; first 18 bytes identical, bytes 18… different, 82 bytes each. Server role: A/B both begin `52b30808615590dca71d0585fb8852bbba66`.
- Impact. A censor records one genuine session and obtains an 18-byte signature per direction that matches every connection in the country using that PSF copy, plus a replayable probe. The "random-looking" carrier is identifiable at line rate.
- Reproduction. Appendix C-3: capture two connections from different clients to one node (or one client to two nodes) and compare the first 18 bytes per direction; replay the first client frame to a node and observe the constant 18-byte reply opener.
- Recommendation. (1) Do not rely on `@SEGMENT.CRYPTO` as shipped. If the PSF cipher is kept it needs a per-connection random salt field sent in clear, a KDF over password and salt (HKDF-SHA256) and counter nonces per direction per session — changes to the vendored interpreter (`chacha.rs`, `vm.rs:345-358`) and the PSF grammar. (2) Alternatively treat the PSF as pure shaping and obtain probe resistance in the carrier: require that the first frame authenticate under a key derived from the server's Noise static key and a time window, and answer failures with server-like silence (RVPN-PRX-004). (3) Refuse to start when a PSF still contains `REPLACE_WITH_PASSWORD`. (4) Rotate the password with client releases and keep it out of logs and crash reports.
- References: ASVS v4.0.3 V6.2.6, V6.2.3, V6.4.1, V2.9.3; CWE-323 (Reusing a Nonce, Key Pair in Encryption), CWE-329, CWE-294 (Capture-replay), CWE-798; RFC 8439 §4.

## 6. Positive Observations

- Nothing is answered in clear: no banner, no decoy, no 101 (`README.md:13-19`; `src/proteus_carrier.rs:339-341`); the probe gate problem of RVPN-SRV-001 is gone, replaced by the narrower behaviours of RVPN-PRX-004.
- PSFs are compiled once at start-up; a PSF that does not parse, compile or exist stops the server naming the file, with the parser's panics converted to errors (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:43-57`, `:165-173`; tested `:527-534`); a configuration without `[proteus] psfs` is refused (`src/config.rs:350-355`, tested `:507-521`).
- Dispatch is explicit and tested: longest fixed prefix wins (`:105-108`, `:137-145`), one prefix-less default at most (`:185-193`, tested `:514-525`), silent peers closed (tested `:466-471`), HTTP request lines claimed by no PSF (tested `:475-493`).
- Shedding runs before the PROXY peek, the first-bytes peek and any allocation (`src/proteus_carrier.rs:320-324`), and refuses silently, which is indistinguishable from an unmatched connection.
- The interpreter's lifetime is bounded: 600 s tunnel budget, 30 s unwind grace, then `JoinHandle::abort` drops the socket halves (`:379-399`); the duplex is 64 KiB per connection (`:30`) and interpreter reads are bounded by the `u16` length fields of both shipped PSFs and a 16 KiB chunk size (`io.rs:117-144`, `net/mod.rs:13`).
- `proxy_protocol` now defaults to `disabled` by enum default and is tested at the `Config` level (`src/proxy_protocol.rs:15-21`, `:398-411`); the parser remains length-bounded (v1 ≤ 107 bytes, v2 body ≤ 64 KiB, versions and field counts checked, `:93-181`).
- Noise NK is unchanged and sound: `Noise_NK_25519_ChaChaPoly_BLAKE2s` via snow with `u16` framing, a fresh responder per rotation key (`src/noise.rs:8`, `:35-55`), forward secrecy from the ephemeral exchange; the README correctly states that confidentiality and server authentication come from this layer, not from the PSF (`README.md:219-227`), and quotes the upstream caution: "Proteus is experimental and not recommended for security-critical applications." (`third_party/proteus/README.md:16-17`).
- The Valkey `Config` `Debug` implementation redacts the password (`src/valkey.rs:33-45`), resolving RVPN-SRV-015 (d); RESP readers clamp bulk, array and depth before allocating (`src/valkey.rs:257-288`); statistics fields are escaped for every line-break form (`src/stats.rs:121-129`).
- The standalone client binds its SOCKS5 listener and UDP socket to loopback only (`src/bin/client.rs:285`, `:990`), requires a PSF (`:267-270`), pins the server's Noise public key (`:109-112`) and compiles the PSF behind `catch_unwind` (`:19-31`).
- Test coverage: 94 unit and e2e tests pass offline; the harness exercises the real carrier with `tls_mimic.psf` in both roles (`tests/harness/src/lib.rs:25-27`, `:44-58`, `:137-143`).
- No `unsafe` in the carrier, the server crate or the vendored Proteus crate; the io_uring backend and its `unsafe` surface are gone.

## 9. Appendix C — Recommended dynamic test plan

Environment: one node built from this tree (`cargo build --release`) in a lab network; a reference nginx with a valid certificate on a second host; the `client` binary from `src/bin`; packet capture on the node's interface; `ss`, `/proc/<pid>/fd`, Prometheus scrape on loopback. Items C-0, C-1 and C-3(a) were executed during the assessment outside the repository and without the server; the rest are recommended.

- C-0 Abort on garbage (RVPN-PRX-001). Executed form: a scratch crate outside the repository with `redsocket-http` and `proteus` as path dependencies, `[profile.release] panic = "abort"`; it compiles `proteus/shadowsocks.psf` (password substituted) for the server role, calls `proteus_carrier::wrap` on an accepted loopback socket exactly as `serve` does, writes `GET / HTTP/1.1\r\nHo` from the other end and awaits the interpreter's `JoinHandle`. Observed: panic at `vm.rs:267:54`; under unwind `is_panic=true`, application side EOF, peer closed with 0 bytes; under abort, SIGABRT, exit status 134. Lab form: start the release server with the example configuration; `printf 'GET / HTTP/1.1\r\nHo' | nc <node> 443`; expected: process exit, every tunnel gone; after the fix, a logged close and `redsocket_hs_fail` unchanged.
- C-1 Passive fingerprint of `tls_mimic` (RVPN-PRX-002). Executed form: parse the `FIXED_BYTES` at `tls_mimic.psf:20` and `:24` with a TLS record/handshake walker (`python3 -I`), extract JA3, randoms, extensions, certificate DER (`openssl x509 -inform DER -noout -text`). Lab form: capture one `client` session and run it through Zeek (`ssl.log`, `weird.log`) and Suricata with the default TLS rules; expected: JA3 `d092a941ea714ee8a125b386f788d07a`, `ssl_invalid_handshake`/application data before Finished, expired certificate, record-length anomalies for transfers above 18 KiB.
- C-2 Active probe (RVPN-PRX-002). Send the 166 bytes to the node and to nginx; expected: 746 bytes back from the node, a TLS alert from nginx. Then send a real ClientHello (`openssl s_client`) to the node; expected: zero-byte close, or abort with a `shadowsocks` default (C-0).
- C-3 Constant opener and replay (RVPN-PRX-003). (a) Executed form: two client-role interpreters on separate loopback connections, each fed a different random 48-byte first write; observed identical first 18 bytes per direction and differing remainders. Lab form: two `client` instances (or two nodes) with the same PSF copy; capture; compare bytes 0-17 in each direction. (b) Replay: re-send a captured first client frame to a node; expected: an 18-byte constant opener in reply (the node's msg2 frame). (c) Nonce reuse: XOR the first frames' ciphertext bodies of two connections and compare with the XOR of their known plaintext structure (Noise length prefix and ephemeral key); expected equality in the known positions.
- C-4 Placeholder password (RVPN-PRX-003 (5)). Start the node with the unedited `proteus/shadowsocks.psf`; expected: start-up succeeds (finding) or is refused (after fix).
- C-5 Behavioural comparison (RVPN-PRX-004). Against the node and nginx: silent connection (record time to close: 5 s vs ~60 s); real ClientHello; `GET` in clear; the 166 bytes followed by one record of random bytes (expect FIN after the record, close at 30 s); with two connections open, send garbage on a third (expect all three reset on an unfixed release build).
- C-6 PROXY stage (RVPN-PRX-006). With `proxy_protocol = "auto"`, 1,000 sockets sending `PROXY ` then stalling; expected: tasks and descriptors held past 5 s and 600 s.
- C-7 Inherited (RVPN-SRV-002, -007, -009, -010, -013): execute tests C-3, C-4, C-6 and C-7 of the redsocket_http report against this node; for RVPN-SRV-008 confirm every socket is gone by 11 minutes (expected here, unlike the predecessor).
- C-8 Logging (RVPN-PRX-007, RVPN-SRV-011). Journals at `warn` and `info` after one session; expected: PSF paths in the banner and one `Interpreter done:` line per connection at `info`.
